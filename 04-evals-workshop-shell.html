Checking AI work before it reaches anyone else.

AI sits between a tool and a collaborator.

A tool does what it is told. A colleague reads the situation and fills in what you left out. This does both, which is why neither habit fits.

We count intelligence in tokens. Tokens say nothing about quality.

Every dashboard shows how much was used. None shows whether the output was good, or who checked it.

Three questions every room asks: which model, how many, did it work?

The first two have answers on a price list. The third is the only one that matters, and it has no list.

The real question is how intelligence takes part in the work.

One piece of work, six questions around it.

What runs it, what it inherits, what it can reach, how much it decides alone, who owns it, how we know it was good. Two of the six are written by the team.

Context is rules, examples and sources, written down.

The rules that never move, three examples of the standard, and where the facts come from. A new colleague needs the same three.

Evals are cases, checks and gates, written down.

A case is a real input with the answer it must get. A check is a question a reader answers yes or no. A gate is what happens when it fails.

Models differ every time, hide their reasons, and err without warning.

Software fails the same way twice. A model can pass on Monday, fail on Tuesday, and sound confident both days.

Say where it should go. Then check where it arrives.

An instruction is an aim. A check is a measurement of the landing. Teams that only write instructions never find out.

A tool you operate forgives a slip. An agent on a long task compounds it.

Watched step by step, an error costs one step. Left to run for an hour, an early error costs the hour, unless a check stops it early.

An instruction hopes. An eval knows.

The same rule, twice: once as a line in the brief, once as a case with the answer it must get. Only the second one can fail out loud.

The same loop at three scales: try, grade, adjust.

Your team runs it on a skill. A lab runs it on a model. The loop is the same size; only what gets adjusted changes.

Task, goal, must never, stop and ask, three checks.

Where a check and your judgment disagree, fix the check.

What gets rewarded gets learned.

Not research and not a measurement.
